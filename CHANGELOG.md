# Purchase Point — changelog

Vehicle extrication for the engine crew, modeled on the vehicle chapter of NFPA 2500 in our own words. Plain-language history, newest first. Practice, not certification; on a call, the maker's rescue guide and your department's procedures come first.

## 0.6.0 — October 18, 2026 — Pedestrian under a car: 20 activities
- A new scenario, the twentieth activity, so every Preconnect module counts 20: a person struck and trapped under a vehicle. Nothing to cut, everything to lift and crib: block the lane, the crowd off the car, chock, crib, the EMS provider at the edge (never under), lifting airbags on a strong point on a cribbed base, lift an inch, crib an inch, never a hand under the load, and when to stop and call heavy rescue.
- Three layouts: a sedan with the patient under the floor pan; an SUV with high clearance, where cribbing it solid and checking the room from the edge brings him out with no lift at all; a patient pinned by a front wheel, where lifting the wrong corner rolls the car onto him and the bags run out of height before he is clear, so heavy rescue finishes the lift while you crib.
- Decision cards, the patient strip (you can hear him, not see him), the crash and the rapid branch, the instructor's injects, Chaos events, teaching cards on every command, and both browser gates.
- Under the hood: cribbing the re-crib spot while the car is shifted always makes it stable, the lift-without-cribbing line names the bag instead of the ram, and the early-entry line uses the patient's pronoun.

## 0.5.0 — October 13, 2026 — Workbench 2: the call sheet
- The tool tray is gone. Every station and scenario is now an officer's call sheet: short commands in the crew's words ("Peel the trim." "Pop the latch." "Roll the dash."), grouped into evolutions in order; you call it, the crew does it, the car shows it. The part chips under the drawing stay as a second way in.
- The tool is asked only where the choice is the lesson: the 12-volt cable (cable cutters, or the big cutters: the wrong-tool lesson), the cutters stalled on boron (the Sawzall), and where the spreader starts on the door. "Remove the door." runs the purchase point, the latch and the hinges as one call.
- A teaching card on every command and every mistake: What, How, Look for, If you find it, Why, with Show me more where there is more. Guided shows a card the first time you call its command; after that, and always in Recall and Chaos, it is behind the ⓘ. The debrief's Learn-from-it chips open the card for each mistake; the lesson and the pocket reference carry every card.
- Words: Sawzall everywhere (a trademark, used as the common term; About says so). Hands is no longer a tool. Battery cable cutters added; the fob is its own step in the power order.
- Nothing underneath changed: every trap, hazard, battery, phase, decision card, wait, inject and score is as it was. Both browser gates tap every command by its visible text.

## 0.2.0 — October 12, 2026 · E2: the car diagram, the Where to cut drill and the five skill stations

- The workbench: one car drawing per run (a side view on its wheels; an end view for a car on its side or its roof), tool first, then the zone. Three bars: stability (stable, rocking, shifted), space (inches gained against the room needed, modeled) and the tool battery. A "what just happened" line that stays. Guided names the next step and marks the hidden hazards; Recall marks nothing; Chaos doubles the cold-weather battery drain, stages no spare, starts on a car the first crew never cribbed, and hides an extra hazard.
- Hidden hazards under the trim: the curtain airbag inflator and tube, the seat-belt pretensioner, boron steel. Peel and peek shows them; a cut without a peel still happens, costs 5 for cutting blind, and an inflator is a never (30). Boron steel stalls the cutter and moves the cut.
- The power-order trap: the 12-volt cut before the windows are down or the locks tried costs 10, and the window becomes a punch, the locks a pry. The reserve-charge line follows every power-down.
- Battery tools with staged spares: a tool dies mid-job, the heavy tools will not run until a spare is swapped in or Engine 10's hydraulic set is called; twenty real seconds with a dead tool costs 5.
- Stability: a heavy tool on a car that is rocking shifts it (10); twenty seconds shifted with nobody cribbing costs 5; the EMS provider sent into a rocking car costs 5.
- Five stations, three layouts each: Stabilize it (wheels, side, roof), Glass (tempered and laminated, laminated side glass, power already off), Make the door (latch side, hinge side, the battery dies), Take the roof (full removal, roof flap, boron steel in the B-post), Dash roll (relief cut and roll, boron steel in the A-post, the battery dies). Lift an inch, crib an inch, measured on every push.
- Where to cut: twelve prompts on the diagram, eight a run, the first tap counts.
- Tap guards on the tray and the zones (rule 4); the person's clocks stop while the screen is off (rule 15); every zone has a 44 px hit rect, a role, a name and a keyboard stop.
- Tests: `car`, `stations` and `cut` sections (45 clean bot runs, every trap, the freeze, the guard, the hit rects) and the browser check plays every station and the drill with real taps; `PC_ALL=1` plays every built activity.
- Deep links: `?station=<id>&v=<layout>`, `?drill=cut`.
